The same mark, working

Three phases, one rig. Thinking is a slow breath with the eyes drifting and the odd blink. A tool call is the tongue: a strike out, a hold on whatever it caught, and a snap back, alternating sides. Typing is the croak — the throat filling in a triple beat, eyes squeezing shut on each, because that is what a toad does when it has something to say.

The body is not a traced outline. It is a jaw ellipse, a crown, two eye domes, two parotoid glands, two foot lobes and six flank warts, all filled the same colour so they union into one silhouette with no seam anywhere. That is why the throat can inflate past the belly line and still look like the same animal: it is another circle joining the union, not a shape being morphed. Nothing here is a gradient, a filter, or a second path drawn to cover a join.

Ports to Glyph.tsx unchanged in shape: rigOf(phase, t) returns one object of numbers — body scale, throat, per-eye lid and gaze, tongue reach — a 300ms eased blend runs between the rig being left and the one being entered, and a single requestAnimationFrame writes transforms. Same architecture as the three dots, one object instead of three.

No external dependencies. Type is the system stack, not Space Grotesk / Geist.
